Blattler Lab

Paleoclimatology and biogeochemistry research in the Blattler Laboratory at the University of Chicago. Work centered on trace metal analysis, carbonate geochemistry, and Early Eocene climate reconstruction from geological core samples.

Lab Blattler Lab, UChicago Conference Midwest Geobiology 2023 Focus Eocene paleoclimate

Overview

The Blattler Lab investigates ancient ocean chemistry and climate through the geochemical record preserved in carbonate sediments. Research in the lab spans a range of deep-time questions — from the chemistry of Precambrian oceans to the stability of Cenozoic climate states — using stable isotopes, trace metals, and radiogenic proxies to reconstruct seawater composition and environmental conditions that predate any instrumental record.

My work in the lab focused on trace metal distributions and carbonate geochemistry in core samples from an Early Eocene interval — a period of sustained warmth roughly 56–48 million years ago that provides an important natural analog for understanding how Earth systems respond to elevated CO₂ concentrations.

Research Focus

Early Eocene Climate Reconstruction

The Early Eocene Climatic Optimum (EECO) represents the warmest sustained interval of the past 66 million years — with global mean temperatures 10–15°C above present and ice-free poles. Understanding what maintained that warmth, and how the ocean-atmosphere system eventually transitioned toward a cooler state, is central to constraining Earth's climate sensitivity.

Carbonate sediments from this interval preserve chemical signals of ancient seawater chemistry — including the Mg/Ca ratio (a temperature proxy), δ¹³C (reflecting the carbon cycle state), and trace element distributions that record diagenetic alteration and original depositional environment.

Trace Metal Analysis

Core samples from Eocene carbonate sequences were prepared and analyzed for trace metal concentrations using ICP-MS. The data were interpreted in the context of diagenetic screening criteria — distinguishing original geochemical signals from post-depositional alteration — and placed within a stratigraphic and paleoenvironmental framework.

Sulfur Isotope Context

The lab's broader sulfur isotope program provided interpretive context for the trace metal work. Sulfur isotope records (δ³⁴S) from the same intervals constrain ancient seawater sulfate concentrations and pyrite burial rates — key variables in the long-term carbon-sulfur-oxygen coupled cycle that regulates atmospheric oxygen on geological timescales.

Midwest Geobiology Symposium 2023

This work was presented as a research poster at the Midwest Geobiology Symposium 2023. The poster presents the analytical framework, trace metal data, and paleoenvironmental interpretation of the Eocene core record.
