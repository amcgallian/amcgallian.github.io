Urban Climate · Field Research · AGU 2024

Environmental Frontiers

Multi-scale urban heat island analysis across Chicago, combining Landsat satellite thermal imagery, mobile sensor networks, GPS tracking, and field surveys. Presented at the American Geophysical Union Fall Meeting 2024.

Period Summer 2024 Conference AGU Fall Meeting 2024 Study area Chicago, IL Scale Satellite + street-level

Overview

Environmental Frontiers is a collaborative urban heat island research project investigating how Chicago's built environment — buildings, parks, pavement, zoning types — shapes local temperature patterns at multiple spatial and temporal scales.

The project fuses two distinct data streams: Landsat 8/9 satellite thermal imagery at 30 m resolution, processed from raw digital numbers through radiometric calibration to land surface temperature; and M-TEMP mobile sensor carts that measure air temperature, infrared, and relative humidity at six vertical heights simultaneously while traversing city transects on foot. GPS tracks tie every sensor reading to a location.

Field campaigns ran from June through September 2024, with teams walking transects across diverse neighborhoods — the UChicago campus, Roosevelt Road, Midway Plaisance, and several parks — collecting paired satellite and ground-truth measurements.

AGU Fall Meeting 2024

Results were presented as a poster at the American Geophysical Union Fall Meeting, December 2024 — one of the largest earth and space science conferences in the world (~25,000 attendees).

Data Sources

Landsat 8/9 TIRS (Satellite)

Thermal Infrared Sensor (TIRS) Band 10 imagery from multiple Landsat overpasses spanning summer 2024. Processing pipeline:

  1. Extract radiometric calibration constants (ML, AL, K1, K2) from MTL metadata using constripper.ipynb
  2. Convert raw digital numbers → at-sensor radiance → top-of-atmosphere radiance
  3. Apply Planck's law: radiance → brightness temperature (Kelvin → Fahrenheit)
  4. Emissivity correction and atmospheric correction for land surface temperature
  5. Resample from 100 m to 30 m resolution; export as GeoTIFF

M-TEMP Mobile Sensor Carts

University of Chicago M-TEMP carts deploy RTD temperature sensors at six heights (0.6 ft, 1.8 ft, 3.6 ft, 5.4 ft, 7.2 ft, 9.0 ft) along with infrared and humidity sensors. Two carts operated in parallel on some transects, allowing side-by-side spatial comparison. Sensor voltages are converted to physical units through calibration curves in the mtemp.py module.

GPS Tracks

Time-synchronized GPS coordinates (latitude/longitude) collected on each walk, merged with sensor readings on common timestamps to create spatially enabled DataFrames. Interpolated onto regular grids using scipy.interpolate.griddata to generate continuous heatmap surfaces.

Field Survey Data

Structured field observations collected via ArcGIS Survey123 — land cover conditions, shade patterns, surface types, and anomalous phenomena — georeferenced to sensor walk routes. Surveys included Roosevelt Road walks (3 days), Florence Stout Park tree survey, and the Midway Landsat Walk (September 8, 2024).

Zoning & Land-Use Data

Chicago community area boundaries and zoning district shapefiles, spatially joined to temperature measurements to analyze how land-use type relates to observed heat patterns.

Analysis

Landsat thermal pipeline (landsattotemp.ipynb)
Full DN → LST conversion chain applied to 10+ satellite scenes across summer 2024.
GPS + sensor merge (sensortomap.ipynb)
Temporal alignment of sensor logs and GPS tracks; spatial interpolation to continuous heatmap surfaces; multi-cart overlay for parallel walk comparisons.
Vertical temperature profiles (vertsprocessing.ipynb)
Analysis of temperature stratification across the six sensor heights, revealing how urban canopy structure shapes the near-surface temperature gradient.
Zoning analysis (zoning.ipynb)
Spatial join of zoning districts with Chicago neighborhoods; identifies dominant zoning class per community area; overlays temperature data for land-use comparison.
Composite aggregation (composite.py)
Aggregates data across multiple test routes and dates into a single composite temperature surface, supporting cross-neighborhood comparison.

M-TEMP Python Module

A core output of Environmental Frontiers is M-TEMP — a reusable, open-source Python package for processing mobile temperature sensor data. The module encapsulates the full data pipeline from raw sensor CSVs through spatial analysis and visualization, making the methodology reproducible and extensible for future mobile sensing campaigns.

M-TEMP provides a command-line interface (main.py) and a core library (mtemp.py) with functions for sensor calibration, temporal alignment, GPS merging, heatmap generation, time series visualization, and scatter plotting. Cart configurations for both Cart 1 and Cart 2 hardware setups are supported.

Open Source

M-TEMP is published as an open-source repository, available for other urban climate research groups to adapt for their own mobile sensing campaigns.
