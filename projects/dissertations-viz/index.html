Data Visualization Fellowship · Cohort Project · 2025–2026

Visualizing UChicago Dissertations, 1893–2025

Interactive four-panel visualization exploring 132 years and ~32,000 dissertations at the University of Chicago — mapping how research topics, disciplines, and global focus have evolved across campus and across the world.

Dataset ~32,000 dissertations Timespan 1893–2025 Context CDS Fellowship cohort project

Overview

The University of Chicago has granted doctoral degrees since 1893. This project asks: what can 132 years of dissertation titles, abstracts, and department affiliations tell us about how a research university's intellectual character changes over time?

Working from a ~32,000-record dataset assembled from ProQuest TDM Studio, the UChicago Library archive, convocation records, and Hathi Trust digitized materials, the project builds a GIS pipeline and interactive four-tab visualization that explores the data from campus geography, global research focus, interdisciplinary networks, and a novel "ring species" methodology adapted from evolutionary biology.

Fellowship Cohort Project

Developed as the cohort project for the 2025–2026 Data Visualization Fellowship at the UChicago Center for Digital Scholarship. View the fellowship →

Data Pipeline

Dataset Assembly

~32,000 records assembled from four sources:

  • ProQuest TDM Studio — bulk dissertation metadata (titles, abstracts, keywords, authors)
  • UChicago Library archive — supplementary descriptive fields
  • Convocation records — historical graduation data to fill department gaps
  • Hathi Trust — early dissertation recovery (1893–1931)

Department Normalization

156 unique department strings across 132 years were normalized to 42 canonical modern departments via an explicit mapping table — accounting for historical renames, joint programs, and administrative reorganizations (e.g., Harris School, Pritzker Molecular Engineering). The mapping rationale is documented per entry.

Geographic Entity Extraction (NLP)

Named entity recognition via spaCy (en_core_web_lg) extracted geographic place names (GPE entities) from titles, subject terms, and keywords. This identified 1,112 unique locations and assigned a primary geographic research focus to 4,068 records (12.7% of the dataset). Top countries: United States (293 dissertations), China (193), France (122), United Kingdom (119).

Extracted locations were geocoded via the Nominatim API (OpenStreetMap) to latitude/longitude for the global choropleth visualization.

Campus GIS

Departments were mapped to their primary campus buildings using a building locations table joined to UChicago property footprint geometries (February 2025). This spatially resolved 6,310 of 32,013 dissertations to specific campus buildings — enabling a choropleth of dissertation output by building.

Visualizations

The interactive visualization presents four analytical perspectives in a tabbed interface:

Tab 1 — Campus Choropleth
Building polygon map of campus colored by dissertation count, with an animated year slider (1959–2025). Reveals how research has shifted spatially across campus over time.
Tab 2 — Global Research Focus
World choropleth showing where UChicago dissertations study — filterable by decade. Shows the university's evolving geographic scope from a US-centric to globally distributed research agenda.
Tab 3 — Interdisciplinary Trends
Line chart of joint dissertation rate over time, stacked bar by division, top 15 department pairs table, and a campus connection map with arc weights proportional to collaboration count.
Tab 4 — Ring Species Analysis
A novel methodology adapted from evolutionary biology: 41 departments arranged clockwise by campus geography, with edges colored by TF-IDF keyword similarity. Tests whether physical proximity predicts intellectual overlap. Scatter plot: spatial distance vs. keyword similarity for all department pairs. Keyword explorer shows TF-IDF top terms per department.

Ring Species — Novel Methodology

In evolutionary biology, a "ring species" is a connected series of populations where neighbors can interbreed but the terminal ends of the ring cannot. Applied here: departments are arranged as a geographic ring, and we test whether adjacent departments are more intellectually similar (higher keyword overlap) than distant ones — and identify the "break point" where the ring's intellectual continuity fails.

Technical Implementation

The GIS pipeline is organized into four Python scripts: dept_housing.py (department normalization + building join), geo_ripper.py (NLP entity extraction + Nominatim geocoding), fill_departments.py (convocation record cross-referencing), and prepare_viz_data.py (pre-computes all visualization datasets into a single 183 KB JavaScript bundle).

The front-end visualization is built in vanilla JavaScript with Leaflet for the spatial views and Chart.js for the statistical charts — no framework, no build step.
