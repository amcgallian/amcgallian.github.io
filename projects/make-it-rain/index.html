Cloud Seeding Efficacy

A literature synthesis and quantitative reanalysis of historical cloud seeding studies, evaluating what the evidence base actually supports.

Course ENSC 36300, Environmental Data Science Capstone Term Spring 2026

Overview

For my MS capstone I evaluated the efficacy of cloud seeding — the practice of introducing substances into clouds to encourage precipitation — by synthesizing the atmospheric science literature and quantitatively reassessing the historical studies most often cited as evidence it works.

The core problem in cloud seeding research is attribution: precipitation is highly variable, seeding is usually applied opportunistically to storms already forming, and many older studies lack the randomized controls needed to separate a seeding effect from natural variability. Much of the project was spent evaluating which claims in the literature actually hold up against that scrutiny, rather than taking published effect sizes at face value.

What this involved

Reviewing decades of published seeding trials and their statistical methodology, then reassessing the strength of the evidence each provides — separating well-controlled randomized studies from opportunistic or poorly controlled ones.
