Environmental Data Science II · Remote Sensing · MODIS

AOD Distribution Changes, 2003–2023

Twenty-year analysis of global aerosol optical depth trends using MODIS Terra and Aqua. Combines distribution analysis, Mann-Kendall trend detection, population exposure modeling, and ARIMA + random forest forecasting to track where aerosol pollution is rising, falling, and who bears the greatest burden.

Timespan 2003–2023 (20 years) Data MODIS Terra + Aqua Course Environmental Data Science II

Overview

Aerosol optical depth (AOD) is the column-integrated measure of how much sunlight is scattered or absorbed by aerosol particles suspended in the atmosphere. It is one of the primary satellite-observable proxies for particulate air pollution — directly linked to PM2.5 concentrations, respiratory health outcomes, and climate forcing.

This project applies twenty years of MODIS satellite retrievals (2003–2023) to answer: how have global AOD distributions shifted over two decades? Which regions show consistent improvement or deterioration? Who is exposed? And what does the near-term trajectory look like?

Data

MODIS Terra & Aqua AOD

The primary dataset combines Level-3 monthly AOD retrievals from two MODIS instruments: Terra (equatorial crossing ~10:30am) and Aqua (equatorial crossing ~1:30pm). Using both sensors mitigates cloud and sampling bias artifacts that affect single-sensor products. Data were accessed via NASA LAADS DAAC and processed using the MOD04/MYD04 550nm Dark Target + Deep Blue merged AOD product.

The 20-year, dual-sensor monthly composites were assembled into a global gridded time-series (0.1° resolution) — approximately 240 time steps per grid cell.

Analysis Pipeline

Distribution Analysis

Global and regional AOD distributions were characterized at decadal intervals (2003–2013, 2013–2023) using kernel density estimation, percentile analysis, and regional boxplot comparisons. Key question: has the global AOD distribution shifted left (cleaner) or right (more polluted) over the study period, and does that shift vary by region?

South Asia and East Africa show rightward shifts (rising AOD), while East Asia exhibits a pronounced leftward shift driven by aggressive Chinese air quality regulations post-2013. Europe and North America trend modestly cleaner throughout.

OLS & Mann-Kendall Trend Detection

Pixel-level linear trends were estimated using ordinary least squares regression on the 20-year monthly AOD time series. Because AOD data often violate normality assumptions, the nonparametric Mann-Kendall test was also applied to each pixel, confirming trend direction and statistical significance without distributional assumptions.

The resulting trend maps show statistically significant AOD decline across much of eastern China and parts of Europe, alongside significant increases across South Asia, Sub-Saharan Africa, and portions of the Middle East — a geographic redistribution of aerosol burden from the developed world to developing regions.

Population Exposure Modeling

AOD trend maps were overlaid with GPW (Gridded Population of the World) v4 population density surfaces to compute population-weighted AOD exposure by region and decade. This translates satellite-observed trends into public health framing: even modest AOD increases in densely populated South Asian cities translate to disproportionately large increases in exposed person-years.

Results show that roughly 2.1 billion people (2020 population base) reside in areas with statistically significant positive AOD trends — disproportionately concentrated in South Asia and Sub-Saharan Africa.

ARIMA Forecasting

Regional mean AOD time series were modeled using ARIMA with seasonal decomposition (SARIMA) to project AOD values 5–10 years forward (2024–2033). The ARIMA order was selected via AIC/BIC on a grid search, with residual autocorrelation checked via Ljung-Box tests. Forecast uncertainty intervals widen appropriately for longer horizons.

Random Forest Prediction

A random forest regression model was trained to predict future AOD using lagged AOD values, land cover type, population density, GDP per capita, and fire activity indices as features. The ensemble model captures nonlinear relationships and interactions that ARIMA misses — particularly the sensitivity of AOD to land use change and economic development trajectories.

Feature importance analysis confirms that lagged AOD and fire activity dominate predictions globally, while GDP-related features become more important in regions undergoing rapid industrial transition.

Key Finding

Global mean AOD has declined slightly over 2003–2023, but this aggregate masks a geographic redistribution: aerosol burden is shifting from East Asia and Europe (improving) to South Asia and Sub-Saharan Africa (worsening). Population-weighted exposure has increased because the highest-growth polluted regions also have the highest population densities.

Policy Implication

The divergent regional trajectories suggest that global air quality progress is uneven and increasingly driven by regulatory capacity rather than technology. Regions without strong air quality frameworks are absorbing the AOD burden that regulated economies have shed.
