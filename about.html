About

GIS and environmental data, based in Chicago.

Download CV

I'm an environmental scientist and GIS developer based in Chicago, with an MS in Environmental Science from the University of Chicago (June 2026). My work splits between fieldwork and code in roughly equal measure — GNSS survey, drone mapping, and geochemical sample analysis on one side, and the Python and JavaScript that turn that field data into usable tools and maps on the other.

The clearest example is this past summer: nine weeks on the geomatics team of an active excavation with the Türkmen-Karahöyük Archaeological Project in Türkiye, running daily GNSS survey and building TKAP Tools, a QGIS plugin the field team still uses to turn raw survey points into stratigraphic maps and section drawings. It's the same pattern across most of what I've built: the Chicago Environmental Atlas maps 150 years of Chicago's ecological history from Landsat and archival data, and a fellowship cohort project traced 132 years of UChicago research output across campus and the world. In between, I've run macroinvertebrate surveys on Lake Michigan revetments, analyzed geochemical cores in a paleoclimate lab, and taught GIS workshops for the university community.

I'm currently looking for roles in geospatial data science, environmental informatics, and GIS development — in Chicago or remote.

Geomatics Research Assistant
