Ecopoetics

Course at the intersection of ecology and literary imagination — reading nature writing, environmental poetry, and field literature, then producing original creative work responding to ecological observation. Final project: a full field guide to a Hyde Park alleyway.

Course Overview

Ecopoetics explores the relationship between ecological science and poetic imagination — how literary language can make environmental observation legible in ways that data alone cannot. The course engaged theoretical and literary influences on noticing and attention — Anna Tsing's The Mushroom at the End of the World, julie ezelle patton's Arkitext, John Muir Laws' techniques for auditory mapping, and Dilip da Cunha and Rachel Havrelock's writing on water and interconnectedness.

As a geospatial data scientist, the course pushed me to work in a radically different register: close observation, attention to the particular, and form as a mode of knowing.

Wandering the Alley: A Guide to a Chicago Alleyway

A field guide to a single alleyway behind a Hyde Park residence — treating an overlooked, transitional urban space as a site worth the same close attention a naturalist would give a forest or wetland. The guide moves through cognitive mapping and the sounds of the alley, its flora, fauna, and fungi, its human traces and waste, its relationship to weather and storm water, and its layered history as part of a portage route once traversed by the Council of Three Fires — closing with a set of rituals for engaging with the space directly.

Written and designed as a complete book — cover photography, front matter, a table of contents, and a full bibliography grounding the project in its literary and ecological influences. Self-published, first edition, December 2024.

Wandering the Alley — Field Guide

35 pages across ten chapters: Introduction, Cognitive Maps, Flora, Fauna, Fungi, History, Geology & the Built Environment, Weather & Water, Human Trace & Waste, and Poison & Extermination — closing with a set of Rituals for direct engagement with the space.
