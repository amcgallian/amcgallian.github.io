Climate Ethics

Philosophical and policy dimensions of climate change — intergenerational justice, geoengineering ethics, and the obligations of environmental scientists.

Course Overview

Climate Ethics explored the moral dimensions of climate change from both philosophical and policy perspectives. The course engaged with foundational questions about responsibility, justice, and the ethics of intervention in Earth systems.

Topics Covered

Intergenerational Justice

Obligations to future generations — who bears responsibility for climate impacts, how to weigh present costs against future harms, and frameworks for intergenerational equity in climate policy.

Geoengineering Ethics

The ethics of deliberate large-scale intervention in Earth systems — solar radiation management, carbon dioxide removal, and the governance challenges of technologies that affect all people regardless of their consent.

Scientific Obligations

The responsibilities of environmental scientists in communicating uncertainty, engaging with policy, and navigating the line between scientific and normative claims.

Climate Justice

Distributional justice in climate impacts and policy responses — how climate change affects vulnerable populations disproportionately and what an equitable response requires.

Course Work

Concept paper exploring a specific ethical dimension of climate change, situating it within broader philosophical frameworks and policy implications.
